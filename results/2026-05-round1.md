# 2026-05/06 bake-off — round 1 (summary)

The first round ran **single-GPU** (one W7800, 48 GB) on an earlier Ollama
(0.24-era), across several waves: classic coder models → the 2025/26 agentic
generation → a 12-model cross-use-case bench → executed HumanEval-164 pass@1.
Its absolute throughput numbers are **obsolete** — the Ollama 0.24 → 0.32.5
upgrade alone later moved decode +30% on the same model/hardware — so this
page records the decisions and durable lessons, not a leaderboard.

## Outcome

**Winner: qwen3-coder:30b (30B-A3B, q4_K_M)** — 93.3% HumanEval-164 pass@1,
~80 tok/s (era), 256K context in ~18 GB, and — decisively — real multi-turn
structured tool-calling. Runners-up by lane: `gpt-oss:20b` for reasoning
(92.5% HumanEval *with* thinking enabled — 59% with it disabled, an early
sighting of the thinking-budget lesson), Gemma 4 family (`26b-a4b`, `e4b`)
for prose.

The 2026-08 round re-benched the winner against nine heavier challengers on
better hardware and it **held** — see
[`2026-08-unified-final.md`](2026-08-unified-final.md).

## Durable lessons from round 1

- **q8 bought nothing over q4_K_M** on these tasks at this model class —
  q4_K_M became the standing quant point.
- **Thinking models invert under `think:false`** (gpt-oss:20b: 92.5% → 59%).
  Disabling reasoning to save tokens costs the capability you kept the model
  for.
- **Tool-calling quality is not implied by pass@1.** Several round-1
  candidates scored well one-shot and could not sustain a structured
  multi-turn tool exchange; the round-2 harness promoted convergence to a
  first-class metric because of this.
- **vLLM quantized serving on RDNA3 was dead at the time** (GPTQ/AWQ ran
  3–5 tok/s via the Triton fallback; Marlin kernels are CUDA-only) — which
  routed the whole serving stack through llama.cpp/Ollama. This was later
  overturned for symmetric GPTQ int4 by vLLM v0.23.0's native RDNA3 kernels:
  see [`../findings/w4a16-native-kernels.md`](../findings/w4a16-native-kernels.md).
