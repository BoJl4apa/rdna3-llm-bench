# 2026-09 round — new challengers, and a rebuilt agentic leg

Six candidates benched 2026-09-02 on the dual-W7800 box: the incumbent daily
driver, its prose/reasoner sibling, and four models released since the 2026-08
round. Engine `ollama/ollama:0.33.2-rocm`, GGUF q4_K_M, temperature 0.2,
16K context, single-card except the 122B (layer-split over the 96 GB pool).

The headline is not the ranking. It is that **the agentic leg from the previous
round was measuring one skill and reporting it as "agentic"** — see
[`../findings/two-agentic-modes.md`](../findings/two-agentic-modes.md), which
is the transferable result of this round. It builds directly on
[tool-loop convergence is a per-run draw](../findings/tool-loop-convergence-instability.md):
that page established the leg needed repeats; this one establishes it needed a
second scenario shape.

affordable — noted as future work, not run here, because raising the cap for
one candidate mid-round breaks the comparison it belongs to.

## What the columns are

- **A. rename** — `legacy-rename-toolchain`: rename a symbol across 12 known
  sites behind `grep`/`read_file`/`edit_file`/`list_files`. Every site is
  discoverable from one grep; nothing is learned by executing anything.
  Complete = all 12 sites edited. **This column measures batching propensity by
  construction:** a strictly serial run needs ≥13 turns against a 12-turn cap,
  so a model emitting one call per turn is scored as failing however diligently
  it works. Three of the six do exactly that (12 calls in 12 turns, every run).
  Read it as calls-per-turn, not as "can rename 12 sites" — the evidence table
  is in the [finding](../findings/two-agentic-modes.md).
- **B. fix-loop** — `staged-rate-limit-fix`: two defects, and `run_tests` is
  **fail-fast**, so the second is invisible until the first is fixed. Complete
  = both defects fixed *and* the suite re-run to green.
- **C. diagnosis** — `null-shipment-diagnosis`: a `NullReferenceException`
  whose throw site is a red herring; the root cause is a builder that never
  assigns the field. Read-only (edits are refused and counted). The answer must
  end in two structured lines naming file and member, so scoring parses an
  answer instead of grading prose.

All three use native structured tool calls, `MAX_TOOL_TURNS=12`, n=5 runs.

## Reading it

**The incumbent held on quality and lost a mode.** 0.99 pass@1 at 96.7 tok/s
is the best quality-per-second in the field, and it leads the rename scenario.
It is also the only candidate that cannot drive the iterative fix-loop: 1/5,
against 3–5/5 for every challenger including the one 12 points below it on
pass@1. Two of its four failures never called a tool at all — it replied with
an analysis instead of an action; the other two fixed the first defect, re-ran
the suite and ground to the turn cap without locating the second. No model in
the field made a wrong edit in this scenario, the incumbent included; what it
lacks is the loop, not the code.

**Speed is no longer where the new models compete.** Two of the four
challengers land at ~42 tok/s — less than half the incumbent — for a pass@1
gain of at most nothing (0.98, 0.97 vs 0.99). The exception is
nemotron-3.5-lightning at **161 tok/s**, 1.7× the incumbent, which is the
fastest decode we have measured on this hardware at this quality level, and it
is a 3B-active MoE. The 2026-08 round's conclusion — *active parameters, not
total, set the speed ceiling on GDDR6 layer-split* — survives another
generation intact: the 122B MoE and the 27B dense land at the same 42 tok/s
from opposite directions.

**The read-only diagnosis scenario separates nobody.** All six candidates,
5/5. It is a *fair* task — the cause does not appear in the stack trace at all, and
a plausible null check sits in an unrelated file — and every model walks it.
Worth publishing precisely because it is a null result: a scenario can be
agentic in shape, multi-turn, and tool-driven, and still carry no signal. We
kept it as a control rather than deleting it.

**Scoring the scenario changed the scenario.** An earlier version of C scored
free prose with a keyword lexicon; laguna-xs-2.1 scored 2/5 with three runs
hitting the turn cap. Rewritten to demand two structured lines, the same model
on the same engine scores 5/5 with zero caps. The lexicon was rejecting valid
paraphrases *and* the prompt's open ending gave a weak model no criterion for
stopping. Structured answers fixed both. Before/after here is a scenario
change, not a model finding — the numbers above are all post-fix.

## Route outcome

The incumbent stays the daily driver. nemotron-3.5-lightning was added as a
**second route for repair-loop workloads** (generate → compile → repair) rather
than being ranked against the driver: at 0.87 pass@1 it is not a replacement,
and at 5/5 on the fix-loop and 161 tok/s it does the thing the driver cannot.
Neither `qwen3.8:27b` nor `qwen3.5-122b` earned a route — both are strictly
slower than an incumbent they do not beat on quality.

## Threats to validity

- **n=5 per scenario, n=1 per HumanEval problem, n=1 hardware.** Enough to
  separate 1/5 from 5/5; not enough to rank 4/5 against 5/5.
- **The scenarios are canned worlds.** Tools operate on in-memory files and
  `run_tests` uses a string oracle over the defect signatures, so a model that
  deletes the offending line reads as having fixed it. They discriminate; they
  do not certify.
- **q4_K_M, one engine version, one temperature, one 12-turn cap.** The cap is
  a scoring parameter: a longer cap converts some capped runs into completions.
- **HumanEval-100 is saturated** at the top of this field (0.93–0.99). The
  spread that matters here is in the agentic columns and in tok/s.
- **One candidate is a floor** (see footnote 1), so the pass@1 column is not a
  clean six-way comparison.
